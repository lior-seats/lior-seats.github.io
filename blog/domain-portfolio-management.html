Domain Portfolio Management: How to Track and Renew Multiple Domains

Centralizing expiry tracking, preventing renewals from slipping through, and keeping a domain portfolio organized across registrars.

A single domain is easy to manage. Once you own five or ten — spread across multiple registrars, registered at different times, with different renewal periods — it becomes genuinely difficult to keep track of what's expiring when. Add team turnover (the person who registered most of your domains left two years ago), and you have a real operational problem.

This post covers practical approaches to managing a domain portfolio, from a simple spreadsheet to automated monitoring.

The multi-registrar problem

Most domain portfolios accumulate across multiple registrars for entirely sensible reasons:

  • One registrar had the best price when you registered a batch of domains
  • Your company acquired another company and inherited their registrar
  • Some TLDs (.io, .co, .ai) were only available through specific registrars
  • A developer registered a project domain on their personal registrar account
  • You moved some domains to Cloudflare for DNS but others stayed at the old registrar

The result: renewal reminders arrive from different email addresses, go to different inboxes, and there's no single view of when everything expires.

The most common way to lose a domain: It was registered by someone who's no longer at the company. Their email address has been deleted or redirected to a general inbox nobody monitors. Renewal reminders go nowhere. Auto-renew fails because the card is no longer valid. The domain lapses.

Step 1: Audit your current portfolio

Before you can manage your domains, you need a complete list. Gather this from:

  • Registrar accounts — log in to every registrar account associated with your company or any current or past team members
  • DNS provider — Cloudflare, AWS Route 53, Netlify DNS, and others show you which domains are configured there (though not all of them are registered through the same provider)
  • Email receipts — search your company email for "domain renewal", "domain registration", registrar names (GoDaddy, Namecheap, etc.)
  • GitHub/code — search your repositories for domain names, CNAME files, CI/CD configs with hostnames
  • SSL certificates — check your active SSL certs for the domains they cover; this is a good proxy for "domains we're actually using"

For each domain you find, record: domain name, registrar, expiry date, auto-renew status, contact email on the registrar account, and whether it's actively in use.

Step 2: Build a central expiry tracker

A simple spreadsheet works for portfolios under 20 domains. A Google Sheet or Notion database with the following columns handles most small-to-medium portfolios:

ColumnWhat to record
DomainFull domain name (example.com)
RegistrarWhere it's registered (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Cloudflare, etc.)
Expiry dateThe actual registration expiry date
Days remainingFormula: =DAYS(expiry_date, TODAY())
Auto-renewYes/No — and whether the payment method is valid
Contact emailEmail on the registrar account (the one that gets renewal reminders)
OwnerTeam/person responsible for renewal
PurposeWhat this domain is used for
StatusActive, parked, redirecting, for sale

Sort by expiry date. Anything under 60 days should be at the top. Review monthly.

Step 3: Consolidate where it makes sense

Having fewer registrars reduces operational complexity. If you have 12 domains spread across 6 registrars, consolidating to 2-3 registrars means fewer login credentials, fewer renewal email streams, and simpler account management.

Good candidates for consolidation:

  • Domains registered with a registrar you no longer actively use
  • Domains where the registrar account is tied to a former employee's email
  • Parked or redirect-only domains that don't need specialized DNS features

Step 4: Set up external monitoring

A spreadsheet is a point-in-time snapshot. For ongoing protection, you need automated expiry monitoring that alerts you without manual updates to a spreadsheet.

External domain monitoring services work by querying WHOIS/RDAP on a schedule and sending email alerts at configurable thresholds. The advantage over registrar reminders: they're independent of your registrar account, send to any email you specify, and alert at multiple intervals.

What to set up for each business-critical domain:

  1. Auto-renew enabled at the registrar with a valid payment method
  2. Registrar renewal reminders going to an active, monitored email
  3. External monitoring alerts at 60, 30, 14, and 7 days — separate email path from the registrar

Checklist: quarterly domain portfolio review

  1. Check all domains expiring in the next 90 days
  2. Verify auto-renew is enabled and payment method is valid on each registrar
  3. Confirm registrar contact email is current and monitored
  4. Review any domains in the "parked" or "for sale" category — drop if not needed
  5. Check that critical domains have external monitoring set up
  6. Verify DNS is still pointing where it should (run a quick DNS check on each active domain)

This takes about 30 minutes per quarter and prevents the scenario where you lose a domain because nobody noticed it was expiring.
