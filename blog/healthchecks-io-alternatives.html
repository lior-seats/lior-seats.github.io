Best Healthchecks.io Alternatives for Cron Job Monitoring (2026)

Compare cron job monitoring tools on price, features, and setup effort — so you can pick the right one for your use case.

Healthchecks.io is the go-to indie tool for dead-man’s-switch cron monitoring — you ping a URL after each job run, and it alerts you if the ping stops. It works well. But at $20–80/month for teams, or when you just need something simpler, lighter, or cheaper, it’s worth knowing what else is out there.

This guide covers the strongest alternatives, what each one costs, and which use cases each fits best.

What to Look for in a Cron Monitor

Before comparing tools, it’s worth being precise about what a cron monitor actually needs to do. The core pattern is the dead man’s switch (also called a heartbeat check): your job hits a unique URL when it completes successfully. If the ping doesn’t arrive within the expected window, the monitoring service fires an alert.

Beyond the basics, the factors that actually differentiate tools are:

  • Alert channels — email is table stakes; Slack, PagerDuty, and webhooks matter for teams
  • Pricing for small teams and indie devs — some tools are aggressively priced for enterprise and painful for solo use
  • Ease of setup — a single curl line added to your crontab is the gold standard; anything more complex creates friction
  • Dashboard and visibility — can you see at a glance which jobs are healthy?
  • Free tier generosity — for side projects, a free tier with even 5–20 checks is genuinely useful

The tools below are evaluated against all five. Healthchecks.io itself is included in the comparison table for reference.

The Alternatives

1. Dead Man’s Snitch ($10–49/mo)

Dead Man’s Snitch is the tool that popularized this category. It’s been running reliably since 2013, which gives it a longer track record than almost anything else in the space.

What it is: A focused dead-man’s-switch monitor. You add a ping URL to your job, set a period, and it alerts you when the ping stops arriving. That’s the whole product.

Pricing: Starts at $10/month for a few snitches; scales up to $49/month for larger teams. No permanent free tier — there’s a trial but you’ll pay after that.

Pros: Battle-tested reliability; clean, uncluttered UI; solid Slack and PagerDuty integrations; one of the simplest setups in the category.

Cons: Less flexible than healthchecks.io — no cron expression validation, fewer notification channels, no self-hosted option. No free tier. Priced higher per monitor than some alternatives.

Best for: Teams that want a proven, no-surprises service and prefer simplicity over feature depth. Willingness to pay a small premium for reliability history.

2. Cronitor (Free–$79/mo)

Cronitor has grown from a simple cron monitor into a broader developer observability platform. It supports cron job monitoring, uptime checks, and a telemetry API for structured run data. If you want more than just a dead-man’s switch, Cronitor is the most feature-rich option in this list.

What it is: Full-stack cron and uptime monitoring with cron expression validation, run telemetry, dashboards, and multi-channel alerts.

Pricing: Free tier available with limited monitors. Paid plans start around $35/month; larger team plans reach $79/month and up.

Pros: Free tier to start; cron expression validation built in; telemetry API for capturing job duration and output; good dashboard for large numbers of monitors.

Cons: More complex than most indie use cases need. Pricing scales fast for larger teams. Not open source.

Best for: Teams that want cron monitoring and uptime monitoring in one place, or that want structured run-level telemetry beyond just ping/no-ping.

3. Sentry Crons (Free with Sentry)

Sentry added native cron job monitoring as part of its core product. If your application already sends errors to Sentry, you can add heartbeat monitoring to your scheduled tasks without paying for a separate service.

What it is: Built-in cron monitoring integrated with Sentry’s existing error tracking, alerts, and dashboards. You instrument your job with the Sentry SDK or ping a check-in URL directly.

Pricing: Included in all Sentry plans. The free tier covers basic usage; paid plans start at $26/month for teams and include cron monitoring alongside errors, performance, and replays.

Pros: No additional cost if you’re already on Sentry; cron failures appear in the same interface as application errors; integrates with Sentry’s existing alert rules and notification channels.

Cons: Only valuable if you’re already a Sentry user — adopting Sentry just for cron monitoring would be overkill. The cron-specific UI is less polished than purpose-built tools. Requires SDK integration for full context (though raw check-in URLs also work).

Best for: Teams already on Sentry who want to consolidate cron monitoring into their existing observability stack at zero marginal cost.

4. Buildkite Scheduled Builds (CI/CD pipelines)

Buildkite isn’t a cron monitor in the traditional sense, but it’s worth including here for teams that run cron-style work through CI/CD pipelines rather than server-side crontab jobs. Buildkite’s scheduled builds feature lets you trigger pipelines on a cron schedule, with native run history, alerting, and retries.

What it is: A CI/CD platform with a built-in schedule trigger. You define a cron expression and a pipeline; Buildkite runs the pipeline on schedule and gives you full run visibility, logs, and failure notifications.

Pricing: Free tier for small teams (25 users, some usage limits). Paid plans start at $35/month for growing teams.

Pros: First-class run history and logs; retry logic built in; alerts on failure; works with any language or runtime; good fit if your “cron jobs” are already structured as CI steps.

Cons: Not a heartbeat/dead-man’s-switch monitor — it runs the job itself, so it doesn’t help if the job runs on a server outside Buildkite. Adds agent infrastructure overhead. Overkill for simple shell scripts.

Best for: Teams whose scheduled tasks are already CI-adjacent — data pipelines, build jobs, automated deployments, test runners — and want scheduling, monitoring, and logs in one place.

5. UptimeRobot + Custom Heartbeat Endpoint (Free, DIY)

UptimeRobot is an uptime monitor, not a cron monitor, but you can approximate the dead-man’s-switch pattern by building a small custom endpoint that your jobs ping, and having UptimeRobot monitor that endpoint’s freshness.

What it is: A DIY approach. You deploy a tiny web service (or a serverless function) that accepts pings from your cron jobs and records the last-seen time. UptimeRobot pings your endpoint and alerts you if it goes down or returns stale data.

Pricing: UptimeRobot’s free tier covers 50 monitors at 5-minute intervals. The endpoint you build costs whatever your hosting costs (often free on Render, Fly.io, or Cloudflare Workers).

Pros: Potentially free end-to-end; flexible — you control all the logic; no vendor dependency.

Cons: Requires building and maintaining a custom ping receiver; UptimeRobot wasn’t designed for this use case, so the alerting semantics can be awkward; not purpose-built means more edge cases to handle yourself; operational overhead increases with job count.

Best for: Developers comfortable with DIY infrastructure who want zero ongoing cost and don’t mind the setup work. Not a good path if you want something running in under 10 minutes.

6. CronWatch (Waitlist, $9/mo)

CronWatch is a purpose-built dead-man’s-switch monitor focused on simplicity and low price. One curl line in your crontab, a configurable grace period, and email alerts when a job misses its window. No infrastructure to manage, no enterprise pricing.

What it is: A lightweight cron job monitor built specifically for indie developers and small teams. The setup is a single curl command appended to your cron job; the dashboard shows which jobs are healthy at a glance.

Pricing: $9/month at launch. Early access available via waitlist now.

Pros: Lowest price in the category by a significant margin; fully hosted with no infrastructure setup; one-line integration; clean email alerts; status dashboard included; free cron expression parser companion tool.

Cons: Not yet fully launched — signing up gets you early access. Fewer notification channels than established tools at launch. Not open source.

Best for: Indie developers, solo founders, and small teams who want the simplest possible cron monitoring at the lowest price. Especially good for side projects where $20–80/month is hard to justify.

When to Stick With Healthchecks.io

Healthchecks.io is genuinely good. If you have multiple cron jobs, need team access, want open-source flexibility, or are comfortable self-hosting, it’s hard to beat. The free hosted tier (20 checks) covers most indie projects entirely. The source code is MIT-licensed and the community is active.

The cases where alternatives make more sense are specific: you’re already using Sentry and want zero-cost integration; your “cron jobs” are really CI pipelines; you want the absolute lowest price for a simple use case; or you find the UI heavier than you need.

Most developers who end up on an alternative do so for price or consolidation reasons, not because healthchecks.io is deficient.

Bottom Line

For most indie developers, the decision comes down to a simple filter: how many jobs do you have, what’s your budget, and are you already using any of these platforms?

  • Already on Sentry? Use Sentry Crons for free.
  • CI/CD-heavy team? Buildkite Scheduled Builds bundles monitoring with execution.
  • Want the proven incumbent at a fair price? Healthchecks.io free tier covers 20 checks; $20/month for more.
  • Want the simplest $9/month option? CronWatch is the new lightweight entrant worth watching.
  • Comfortable with DIY? UptimeRobot plus a custom endpoint gets you there for free.

Whatever tool you pick, adding even a single-line heartbeat to your cron jobs is one of the highest-ROI reliability improvements you can make. Silent cron failures are common, detectable, and entirely preventable.
